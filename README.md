# Prodintro.com — Launch videos for SaaS, products and websites

Prodintro.com is a portfolio project: a SaaS-style web app that turns a text prompt into a cinematic motion-graphics video. You can use it for intros, trailers, launch promos and social reels.

- **Website → intro**: paste a URL and Prodintro.com imports the site's name, tagline, feature headlines, stats, call to action, header logo, screenshots, product videos and brand colours, then storyboards a launch video around them. The video follows a hook, logo, a product tour of the real UI, a features bento, integrations, and a CTA with the site's own button label. Copy is always claim-safe (see below), automatically, with nothing to switch on or confirm.
- **SaaS launch-video mode**: modelled on popular product-launch videos. It uses sentence-case blur reveals with gradient accent words, rotating word swaps, a cursor-driven UI zoom tour with callouts, bento feature grids with live micro-animations, floating glass UI widgets, pain-to-solution strikes, an integration orbit, real testimonials, a customer-logo marquee and a CTA button that the cursor clicks. It has a grid, spotlight and beam backdrop, spring physics and glass cards with animated border beams, and is scored with an upbeat track plus UI sound effects synced to every click, pop and whoosh.
- **New slides are written for your video**: a slide added in the studio isn't sample text. The built-in director writes its version of that slide from the same website, listing or prompt (a few story angles), preferring wording the video doesn't use yet. When the director wouldn't use that slide, a slide in the same story role lends its words, or the slide is built from the video's own brand and features (*Inside Nimbus* with Tasks, Docs, Chat).
- **Placeholders where your pictures go**: a slide that shows your pictures but has none yet (no image on the slide, none from the site) shows a placeholder graphic in their place, in the videos and in the skill gallery, slide-style picker and template samples: a dashed *Product image* tile (the product slides cut it out and stage it like a real photo), an app screen, a website page, or a dashed *Image* frame. The slide editor marks it *Placeholder* with an **Add image** button, and Export asks before exporting a video that still has placeholders. Placeholders are drawn in the browser and never saved in a video.
- **Preview size**: the studio's player has S, M, L and Fit sizes (remembered) and full screen (or double-click the video).
- **Editorial system slides**: twelve slides in the style of an interface designer's motion work. They are built from one closed system: the palette's colours, one card surface and three tiers of hairline (frames, dividers, the background grid). The hairlines draw first, surfaces fill in behind them, then type rises out of its line masks. *Kinetic Poster* is a Swiss poster that builds itself: frame and column rules, huge stacked type with the accent in italic serif, an accent disc with a turning tick ring, and a numbered list column. *Showreel* is a carousel of framed cards that snap to centre one by one, with corner ticks on the card in focus and a counter and progress hairline underneath; it uses the brand's photos when there are any. *Card System* shows a poster tile, a live chart card (no numbers, so it makes no claims), a checklist and a brand card. *Kinetic Rows* puts the line between bands of big type sliding in opposite directions. Eight more extend each family. Two more posters: *Grid Poster* is a Bauhaus 3×3 module grid whose squares fill with shapes on the beat and turn while it holds, and *Split Poster* is an accent panel with the brand's monogram rising inside it, beside the stacked headline, feature chips and a running ruler. Two more ways through the features: *Card Stack* is a fanned deck whose top card is swiped away on each beat, and *Contact Sheet* shows every feature on one sheet with a focus frame moving between them. Two more card layouts: *Spec Sheet* is a ruled table where each feature lands on its row with its detail and a tick, and *Widget Set* has live controls (a switch, a ring that fills and ticks, teammates, a timeline), with no numbers shown. Two more kinetic type slides: *Type Echo* holds the line still while outlined echoes of it run as endless marquees above and below, sliding in turn as the stack scrolls upward on a loop, and *Type Slots* spins each word on its own slot reel through the video's other words until it locks. Remakes rotate the carousels and card layouts into the feature slot, and set the positioning line as one of the posters or kinetic type slides. The Kinetic, Editorial, Swiss, Brutalist and Enterprise styles use them by default. The brand bug steps aside on the full-frame type slides, which carry their own header.
- **Movie trailers**: describe a video ("Horror movie trailer for \"The Hollow House\": a family moves into a haunted farmhouse… directed by Jane Marsh, starring Ava Cole and Ben Ortiz, in cinemas October 31") and the trailer is cut like a real one: a **studio ident** (a beam of light sweeps across the studio's name, *presents*), **title cards** between the shots ("This October", the story told one line at a time), the video's title, and a **billing block** (the title over condensed credits and the release line). Twelve genres set the palette, title type, tempo, cuts and how the title cards move: *Horror* (flickering, jittering cards), *Thriller* (a slit of light), *Action* (slammed in with a flash), *Sci-Fi*, *Fantasy*, *Drama* and *Romance* (slow fades), *Comedy* and *Family* (colour cards that pop), *Film Noir*, *Documentary* and *Western*. Movie trailers play in 2.39:1 widescreen with letterbox bars. Credits come only from the prompt (studio, director, writer, cast, release date); nothing is invented, and without them the billing block shows the title and release line alone. The trailer style picker shows *Movie trailers* first and *Brand & launch trailers* below; Auto picks a genre when the prompt reads as a video (video, movie, series, anime, documentary…) and a brand style otherwise.
- **Auto picks the best style for each video**: a style you pick (a SaaS style or a trailer style) belongs to the video you picked it for: it stays through that video's remakes, alternative takes and reloads, and a style picked since the last Generate is used for the next video, even when the prompt was typed or changed after picking it. The next video made from another website or prompt goes back to *Auto* and gets the style that suits its purpose: online stores and corporate, B2B or professional-services sites get *Enterprise Clean* (law and finance firms *Swiss Clean*), AI companies get the dark *AI Glow*, and other products their category's style (developer tools *Ink & Light*, security *Sci-Fi HUD*, health *Minimal Light*…).
- **Photo card slides**: three slides for image-led products (stores, travel, food, fashion, creative work, physical products). *Photo Fan* sets the photos as portrait cards on an arc that turns one card per beat: the centre card upright and largest, the others tilting away along the curve, smaller and dimmer, opening out of a stack and closing back into it. *Card Spread* fans a stack open like a hand of cards, then lifts each photo out in turn with its caption. *Photo Drop* drops white-bordered prints onto the stage one after another, each at its own slight angle, with handwritten-style captions. They use the scene's own image, the brand's photos and captured UI (product photos on their studio cards). For photo-led sites, remakes rotate the gallery between the 3D Carousel and these three, and product videos rotate their gallery through them too.
- **Context-aware bento tiles**: each Bento Grid tile picks its icon and its small live visual from its own words. The title's head noun leads ("Team *calendar*" is a calendar, "Fast *checkout*" a card), then the rest of the title, then the tile's one-line description, then modifiers like "fast" or "live". A tile whose best icon is already on screen gets a related one (another chart, another calendar) before a generic one. The visual follows the subject too: bars or a sparkline for analytics and money, teammates for collaboration, a checklist for tasks and approvals, a switch for settings, access and alerts, a ring that fills and ticks for goals and launches (no percentages, so no made-up numbers). Feature icons, Showreel, Card Stack, Contact Sheet and Feature Slides use the same matching.
- **Your intros sidebar** (studio, left): your intros newest first, the open one highlighted, with a search box once there are more than six; **+ New intro** at the top (an empty studio, the description box ready) and **All intros** (your account page) at the bottom. Signed in, it lists the intros saved to your account (with their thumbnails) plus ones made in this browser that aren't saved yet; signed out, the ones made in this browser (up to 30, newest kept; *Sign in to save them to your account*). Each Generate or Import starts a new entry, and remakes and edits update it. Opening an intro fills the Create fields back in with what it was made from: the website or listing link (and the imported site's card, so remakes and story changes work from it again), the description, product photos, length and story (kept with the intro in this browser, and on your account when it's saved there; uploaded photos past the 48-hour clean-up drop off the list). Each intro's **⋯** menu (or a right-click) can **Rename** it (the name stays as the video changes, in this browser and on your account), **Duplicate** it ("Name copy", saved to your account too when signed in and there's room) or **Delete** it after a confirm (from your account or this browser; deleting the open intro starts a new one). It folds to icons only with its button or ⌘/Ctrl+B (hovering an icon names the intro), and the choice is remembered on this device; until you choose, it starts open on wide screens (1440px and up) and folded on smaller ones. Hidden on phones and narrow tablets, where there's no room.
- **Description meter**: under *Or describe it*, a bar fills towards a good length (60+ characters: the name, what it does and 2–3 features) with how many characters are still missing, then a tip to list features when it finds fewer than two, or what it found (*✓ Good: Ledgerly, 3 features found*). The count shows *n/1000*, switching to *n left* in the last 100 (the director reads the first 1,000 characters, so the box stops there). With a website imported, the box is optional extra direction and only counts.
- **Remake: design or story**: *Remake* opens two choices. **Remake design** keeps the story exactly as it is (slides, words, pictures, timing and your edits) and moves each slide to another design of its family (openers, logo reveals, positioning lines, feature lists, services, overview cards, process layouts, product screens, galleries), instantly in the browser; product moments, problems and the end card keep theirs. **Remake story** tells it a new way: the next story angle (Problem → solution, Before → after → bridge, Product-first, Proof-first, Story-led) that the material supports, checked first with the built-in director (problem-led needs problems to open on, proof-first needs real proof, and the new telling must differ from the one on screen). Trailers draw other slides for the same beats. Each one is kept as a version (*Design 2*, *Story 3 · Product-first*…), so Undo and Original go back.
- **Remakes look different**: each *Remake* tells the same material with other slides. Besides the director's own rotations (story angle, feature layout, product moment, tour, gallery and positioning line), a remix pass moves each section to another design that takes the same content: the opener (Blur Reveal, Type Cascade, Split Wipe, Type Echo, Style Shuffle), the logo reveal (the 3D and clean logo intros, also without a logo image), the feature list (Icon Features, Showreel, Card Stack, Contact Sheet), the overview cards (Bento, Card System, Spec Sheet, Widget Set) and how it works (six process layouts). Each section is offset differently, so consecutive remakes rarely share more than one or two slides. Trailer remakes draw other slides too. The original keeps the director's best fit.
- **Stop the director**: while it works, *Generate* becomes *Directing… ■ Stop* (and *Remake*, *Import* and *3 more takes* become Stop buttons too). Stop cancels the request, every button goes back to its default, and the video on screen stays exactly as it was (a late answer is ignored).

A product video's *Epic trailer* offers the five Product Trailers styles instead.

- **3D logo intros**: a section of eight brand reveals that turn the logo into a solid 3D object and stage it like a film studio's ident, landing on the music's drop. The logo's own silhouette is stacked along a depth axis behind its glossy face, darker towards the back, lit by the turn, with a bright bevel, so any shape extrudes: a square mark, an app icon or a wide wordmark. With no logo, the site's app icon is used, else a generated brand mark. They share one ident kit:
  - **Light**: volumetric shafts of light fanning out from behind the mark, a glow around its own shape, a sweep of light across its face, a thin anamorphic streak and a lens flare with ghosts on the hit.
  - **Space**: a perspective floor with grid lines gliding towards the camera and a glowing horizon, the mark's reflection in it, and a soft contact shadow.
  - **The drop**: energy streaming in from the edges of the frame before it, then a floor shockwave, hot sparks that cool to the brand colour and bounce off the floor, and a camera kick.
  - **Life**: embers drifting up (fine ones far back, big out-of-focus ones near the lens) that the blast pushes away, a camera that rushes in and creeps on while the logo holds, and a slow turn so its depth keeps reading.
  - **The name** rises in below, tracking in with a sweep of light (left off under a wordmark logo, which already spells it).

  Each one has its own moment too. *3D Extrude* builds the logo with a scan line, then snaps it into a solid block. *Coin Spin* spins it like a coin, glinting edge-on. *Shard Assemble* flies thick, light-rimmed shards in from depth, and their seams glow as they fuse. *Orbit Rings* circles it with three tilted rings of comet lights that pass in front and behind. *Hero Stage* raises it through a rippling, reflective floor under a dusty cone of light. *Layer Stack* collapses glinting glass plates into one solid mark. *Fly-through* warps down a tunnel of its glowing outlines. *Tile Flip* flips it in thick strips with a glint running down the wave. Nothing flashes the frame: measured at 0 flashes per second, with light ramping in and out rather than pulsing. The 3D styles use them as their reveal: Epic Launch → Hero Stage, 3D Spatial → Layer Stack, 3D Turntable → Coin Spin, 3D Glass Slab → Tile Flip, Liquid Chrome → 3D Extrude, Blockbuster → Shard Assemble, Warp Drive → Fly-through, Deep Space → Orbit Rings (the 3D styles' tilted panel is skipped for them, since they stage their own 3D). Remakes cycle through them, and trailers can open on one. They sit in their own *3D logo* group in the slide picker.
- **Animated background shapes or watermark text**: SaaS slides get shapes floating around the edges of the frame in the palette's colours, from a set you pick under *Style → Background shapes*: *Geometric* (rings, triangles, hexagons, dot grids; the default), *Soft* (blobs, pills, bubbles, squiggles), *Tech* (code brackets, braces, chevrons, a cursor, linked nodes, a prompt), *Sparkles* (sparkles, stars, twinkles) or *Lines* (waves, arcs, dashed rings, spirals). Or pick *Watermark text*: your line (the brand's name by default) in big, faint outlined rows drifting across the frame, alternate rows in opposite directions. Shapes drift and turn on the video's own clock, so they carry on across cuts instead of jumping, sit at different depths, breathe gently and swell a touch on each kick of the score. Every option previews live in the picker, *Off* gives a clean stage, and the choice is remembered and saved with the video and its share link.
- **Cinematography pass**: after a style is applied (to a new video, a remake or a style switch), a director-of-photography pass gives the storyboard a last look so the cut reads well for the brand. The video opens clean, the logo reveal arrives on a confident move (dolly, push or dissolve, never a busy glitch or split, since it lands on the drop), and the end card arrives calmly. Two big transitions never run back to back (the second becomes a cut on the beat), and one transition never plays three times in a row. The reveal and end card get enough time to read, and the same slide never plays twice in a row. It doesn't touch the story or the copy, and running it again changes nothing. Across 540 storyboards (45 styles × 4 products × 3 lengths), it took grammar slips from 561 to 0.
- **Square cards in two columns**: in 1:1 videos, feature card slides (Feature Icons, Bento Grid, Contact Sheet and Widget Set) lay out in two columns like vertical ones, instead of squeezing four into a row, so the cards and their text stay large.
- **Product tours that fit every format**: in vertical (9:16) and square (1:1) videos, the UI Zoom Tour and Spotlight windows take the screenshot's own shape, so the whole product shows instead of a cropped middle. The tour zooms closer in tall and square frames (until the chosen component fills the width), and vertical tours list their stops as numbered steps under the window, lighting up as the cursor reaches each. Exploded UI grows as it tilts so it fills tall and square frames, with its labels stacked underneath.
- **Character slides**: friendly flat cartoon people present the message. *Hello Mascot* pops up, waves and says the headline in a speech bubble; *Presenter* points at each point on a board as it ticks in; *Team Hello* is a small team where each in turn waves and says one point; *Aha Moment* shows a worried character thinking about the problem until a light bulb comes on and it jumps for joy as the answer appears; *At the Desk* types at a laptop while updates pop up beside it, then cheers; *Cheer CTA* has two characters jumping either side of the call to action under gently falling confetti. One posable character (head, hair, face, arms and legs from simple shapes) is dressed in the video's colours with a varied cast of skin tones and hair, blinks and moves on its own, and nothing flashes. They're in the slide picker under *Characters*. Six *Cartoon* styles tell a whole video with them, each drawing the characters its own way (flat, comic ink, storybook pen or soft clay); one is picked automatically when a description asks for a cartoon, characters, a mascot or kids, matched to its words (comic or funny → Comic Pop, story or bedtime → Storybook, clay or toys → Clay Buddies, night or dreams → Night Owls, park, garden or nature → Sunny Park, else Cartoon Pals).
- **Advanced characters**: a jointed 2D rig for explainer-style videos (slide picker: *Advanced characters*). A skeleton of hips, spine, neck, shoulders, elbows, wrists, knees and ankles drives tapered limbs at real proportions, and the character turns from the front to a side profile, so it can walk: thighs and shins swing, knees bend on the swing, arms counter-swing and the body bobs. Faces have eye whites, irises, pupils, catch-lights and eyelids (blinks and eye darts), brows that act, a nose, and lips that smile, frown or open into talking shapes; hands wave, point, make a fist or a thumbs-up; outfits have collars and open jackets; hair styles include a ponytail that swings, and some wear glasses or a beard. Six slides: *Walk-in Host* (walks in, turns to camera, waves and introduces the headline), *Explainer* (points at each point on a floating card, talking and nodding), *Conversation* (two characters talk your lines as an alternating conversation, the listener nodding), *Problem Solver* (ponders, hand to chin, as problems are crossed out, then has the idea), *Hold-up Sign* (walks in holding a sign with the headline above the head) and *High Five* (two characters walk in, high-five and cheer as the call to action pops up). *Explainer Studio* and *Story Cast* tell a whole video with them; a description asking for an animated explainer or a presenter picks Explainer Studio.
- **Abstract characters**: a modern, minimal character library that makes up a new cast for each video (slide picker: *Abstract characters*). A remake picks a new cast. - **Characters in Cartoon styles**: the Cartoon styles stay few; the characters are a choice inside each one. Under the style picker, *Characters* shows a live preview of each option in the video's colours and the style's drawing look: *Style's own* (its usual cast), *Abstract* (geometric noodle people), *Memphis* (the modern Corporate Memphis look), *Blob* (cute one-shape mascots with big eyes and stubby legs), *Stick figure* (minimal: a round head with a simple line face that shows its mood, and line limbs in one colour, no hair or clothes) and *Classic* (a traditional 1930s rubber-hose cartoon with white gloves and pie-cut eyes). Faces show feelings: eyes with a glint, brows that lift, happy closed eyes when they wave or cheer, wide eyes and an "o" when surprised, a raised brow when thinking. **Lip-sync**: with a voice-over on, the character who speaks the story (the mascot, the presenter or host, the speaker in a conversation, a parent in the 3D home slides) mouths the narration: the mouth opens with the voice's loudness and takes the shape of the sound (open for a, wide for e and i, round for o and u, closed for m, b and p), read from the voice-over's own audio and word timings, so preview and export match. **Character voices**: speaking characters get a voice of their own, separate from the narrator's (a host or mascot one voice, the presenter another, the parents in the home slides a mum's and a dad's voice), and the two in a conversation take turns line by line in two different voices, each lip-syncing only to their own; *Character voices* in the Voice tab turns it off for one narrator throughout. - **Character designer**: design your own simple abstract characters at `/characters` (also *Characters* in the site menu) or in the studio under *Style → Characters*. Pick a type (*Abstract*, *Memphis*, *Blob*, *Stick figure* or *Classic*; options a type doesn't use are hidden and labels fit it, like Pear, Gumdrop and Ghost for blobs), a drawing style (*Flat*, *Soft 3D*, *Outline*, *Line art* or *Paper cut*), a body shape and pattern, a head, a hair style and a face (dot, line or oval eyes, glasses, rosy cheeks, a nose), then the colours (any colour, or the swatches, which include your brand's) and the proportions (body width and height, head size, neck, leg length). Options are shown as small pictures of your character with that choice, next to a live preview that idles, blinks and waves. *Shuffle* invents a new character in the same style, *New colours* keeps the shapes, and *Download PNG* saves a transparent 1024 × 1280 image. Keep a cast of up to 8 (add, duplicate, reorder, remove); the first is the lead. The cast is kept in your browser and saved with the video: the abstract character slides cast yours first (the lead says hello and starts the conversations; the others stand front and centre in crowds), then generated people fill in, drawn in your lead's style. *Random* in the studio switches back to a generated cast.
  - **Colours that match the intro**: characters, generated or yours, are dressed in the video's own colours: its primary, secondary and accent, their tints and shades, neighbouring and complementary hues and a neutral, each nudged until it stands out from the background. You can always choose your own: picking any colour in the designer switches that character to *My colours* (kept in any video), and *Match the intro* switches it back. In the studio the designer previews your characters in the video's colours; *New colours* tries a new mix of them. Generated people otherwise follow the video's style (outlined in Comic Pop, line art in Storybook, soft in Clay Buddies, paper cut in Memphis Crowd).

  - a kanban board where a cursor drags a card and the rest of the work flows into Done (projects, hiring and sales pipelines);
  - live cursors: named teammates move, lasso and comment on a shared board (collaboration tools);
  - a chat thread with typing indicators, a reaction and the product's own update card (messaging and support);
  - *Keycaps*: shortcuts pressed on big keycaps, the action each one runs springing up under them (keyboard-first products);
  - *Toggle List*: a cursor switches settings on one by one, then "Settings saved" (settings, permissions, controls);
  - *Changelog*: a "What's new" feed whose entries roll in with New and Improved tags (release notes, product updates);

  - *Inbox Sweep*: messages swipe away one by one to an empty, calm inbox (email and triage);
  - *Comment Pins*: comment pins pop onto the product's own screen, threads open with an @mention, one is resolved (feedback and review);
  - *Table Fill*: a table fills cell by cell (names, status pills, owners, tags), then sorts by status (databases, spreadsheets, CRMs).
  - *Phone Tour*: the product on a phone: the screen scrolls, fingertips tap, a notification drops in and callouts pop out beside the taps (mobile apps: iOS, Android, "on the go");
  - *Drop Zone*: a file is dragged into a drop zone, a progress bar runs, and the results pop out as cards (upload, PDF, convert, transcribe and summarise tools).

  The director picks the moment that suits the product best. Each moment is scored on the words the site itself uses (its tagline and description count double), the moment its category's videos typically stage, and what the site has to show. A deploy platform gets code to deploy, a whiteboard gets live cursors, a CRM its deal pipeline, a hiring tool its candidate board, and a support inbox a customer chat. The AI director is briefed with the same ranking. Hover a beat in the story-arc strip to see why it was chosen ("Best fit: the site talks about deals, pipeline").
- **Continuity transitions**: six transitions that make one slide turn into the next, so a video reads as one continuous move. *Morph* is a match cut: the outgoing slide pushes in and softens as the next settles out of a slight zoom, so centred headlines flow into each other; *Portal* opens the next slide in a window in the middle of this one and flies the camera into it; *Iris* opens it in a growing circle edged with a thin brand-colour ring; *Spin* turns both slides about the centre with motion blur; *Split* parts the slide down the middle; *Card swipe* shrinks the slide into a card and swipes it away. They flash nothing full-frame (measured at 0 flashes a second on dark, light and colourful styles), are in each slide's transition list, and many styles mix them in (Midnight, Keynote, Kinetic, Spatial, Pop, Gradient Flow and others).
- **Liquid transitions**: the next scene rises in behind a wavy liquid front, with blobs racing ahead that merge into the surface and a refracting meniscus. It's used by the Liquid Motion style and available in any video's transition list.
- **Clean logo intros**: eight flat, minimal brand reveals, the calm counterpart to the 3D set, built on the classic motion-design logo stings: *Line Draw* (the outline draws on like a pen, then the logo fills from the centre), *Wipe Lock-up* (a brand-colour bar uncovers the logo, which slides aside as the name slides out from behind it), *Pop & Burst* (a dot bursts into a ring and the logo pops in with dashes and confetti shapes), *Dot Morph* (a dot drops, bounces and stretches into the logo), *Slice Build* (slices slide in from alternate sides), *Dot Grid* (dots in the logo's own colours merge into it), *Type Lock-up* (the name types itself out beside the logo) and *Shape Assemble* (a circle, square and triangle orbit and collapse into it). The music's drop lands mid-build and the logo resolves a beat later. Clean, light and editorial styles use them (Paper, Frosted, Enterprise, Swiss, Clay, Editorial, Kinetic, Mono, Ink, Daybreak), and remakes alternate them with the 3D intros.
- **Process and services slides**: *Process Arrows* (business-process chevrons work like tabs: each fills with the brand gradient and lifts as it becomes active, a marker points at it, and the active stage's name appears large under them, animated word by word with its detail, so viewers always know which step is being told), *Process Cycle* (steps around a ring, a glowing head running step to step and closing the loop), *Step Ladder* (a 3D staircase rises and a marker hops up it to a flag on the top step) and *Services* (a large circle shows the current service's icon beside the list of services, the highlight moving down the list). Items read "Stage — short detail". Epic versions stage them bigger, each naming the current step or service large: *Step Portals* (the camera flies through glowing rings, one per step, with light streaks), *Light Trail* (a comet races along a sweeping path and ignites each step), *Flip Cards* (numbered cards flip to reveal each step, with a glint), *Service Orbit* (services orbit a glowing core and swing round in turn to 3 o'clock, facing their name, or to the front in vertical videos), *Service Carousel* (a cover-flow of service cards under spotlights) and *Hex Grid* (a honeycomb whose current tile rises in 3D under a beam of light). Remakes rotate how-it-works through the line, process arrows, light trail, staircase, portals, cycle and flip cards; service businesses (agencies, studios, consultancies) get a services slide (list, orbit, carousel or hex grid) and lead with the process arrows.
- **Creative services slides**: *Service Cube* (a big 3D cube turns a quarter turn per service, with its name told large beside it), *Service Bloom* (services open as petals round the brand mark; the current petal stretches out and glows), *Card Fan* (services held as a hand of cards; the current card lifts out and grows to fill the stage), *Departure Board* (an airport split-flap board flips into the service names, the current row lights up with a NOW tag and a ticker types its detail), *Bento Focus* (the big bento box shows the current service and changes to the next, while a tile per service stays put beside it like tabs, the current one lit) and *Service Spotlight* (a stage light swings from plinth to plinth and the lit service rises under its name). The director rotates them with the other services slides for service businesses.
- **UX pass (30+ fixes)**: a menu button on phones (the site's links were missing there) and the current page marked in the header; the brand name read from "Name: …" prompts first (a prompt ending "for SaaS teams" no longer names the product "SaaS"); claim-safe site copy (no "in under a minute" or "Instant"), consistent "Try it free" buttons and page titles, and pricing that says the 1080p the studio exports; balanced headings with no lone last word; a branded "page not found"; a focus ring on the hero's address field; in the studio, a clear message for input that isn't a web address, a short placeholder, a "Sample video" badge for the sample, a readable dismissible director's note, the claim-safe note folded to one line, slide numbers on narrow timeline segments, and on phones a header that scrolls away, no size switch, Undo and Redo on one line and the new video scrolled into view after Generate; on the account page one plan card while Pro adds nothing, plain usage counts, Show/Hide inside the password field and "Sign out everywhere"; palettes in one sideways row on phones; real page headings, labelled fields, unique logo gradient ids and bigger "Use this skill" targets.
- **Mobile first**: on phones the studio follows the order you use it: the video at the top (with the director's note under it), then the settings, then the timeline, versions and slides. Generate, Remake and Export sit in a bar fixed at the bottom within thumb reach (clear of the home bar on notched phones). Fields use 16px text so phones don't zoom in on tap, touch screens get finger-sized controls (about 40–44px), small print stays at 12px or more, the home page's skill cards swipe sideways in rows, and every page fits 320, 360 and 390px wide screens without the page zooming out. The site fills the screen edge to edge (`viewport-fit: cover`) and pinch-zoom stays on. Desktop is unchanged.
- **3D arrows**: a chunky extruded arrow in the brand gradient, with a gloss edge and soft shadow, that draws itself on. Problem → Solution uses it between each problem and its answer. The *3D Arrow* slide sweeps a big one up across the frame past the steps as milestones (the Pop style's how-it-works, and every third remake with short steps).
- **Captions never cover the slide**: with voice-over captions on, every slide plays in the frame above a caption band (its photos, headings and cards a little smaller and higher), and the band carries the slide's colours down with a soft reflection, so the captions sit on it without overlapping a heading. Listing titles become short headings too ("T9 Vintage Men's Electric Hair Clipper", not the listing's whole keyword string).
- **3D mouse pointers**: the pointer in product demos, tours and buttons is a large, modern 3D pointer (a rounded arrowhead with depth, gloss and a soft shadow) that glides with eased, hand-like motion, leans into its moves and presses into the screen on a click. Pick its look under Style → Mouse pointer: *Auto* (white on dark styles, graphite on light), *White*, *Graphite*, *Brand* (your gradient with a glow), *Glass*, *Clay* or the *Classic* arrow. The choice is remembered and saved with the video.
- **Motion blur**: a film camera's 180° shutter. Each exported frame averages six renders across the shutter, so fast moves streak along their path while still parts stay pin-sharp, and samples never cross a cut. It's applied to the final render (the export) only; the preview stays sharp. Switch it off under Style → Motion blur.
- **Story-beat slides**, added when the material is there:
  - *Who It's For*: audience chips (Designers, Developers, Marketers) light up in turn and a card swaps to show what each one gets. Added when the site itself names two or more of the people it's for;
  - *What's in the Box*: for product videos, the box opens with a glow and its contents rise out one by one, as icon tiles with their names. Added when the listing says what comes in the box ("In the box: …", "Comes with …"); quantities are dropped.
- **Classic launch-video slides**, each used only when the site gives it material:
  - *Support*: a help centre where a question types into search and articles appear, then the support chat widget answers (when the site mentions support, docs or onboarding; no response-time claims);
  - *World Map*: a flat dotted map with pulsing pins, flying arcs and live event cards (instead of the globe when the site talks about countries, regions, currencies or languages);
  - *Feature Slides*: one full slide per feature with its number, icon, title, benefit and the product's own UI, with story-style progress bars (long videos with real feature descriptions and product imagery);
  - *Problem → Solution*: each of the site's pains is struck through and answered by the feature that solves it (pairs matched by shared words and topic, e.g. "Lost receipts → Receipts matched automatically"); otherwise the before/after slider.
- **Scene moments**, used only when the site gives them material: a dotted globe that turns while arcs fly between cities and land with live events (only when the site talks about global use), and a before/after slider that drags from a grey, cluttered "old way" (the site's own pains) to the product screenshot.
- **The real logo, whatever it's made of**: the brand mark is found in the site header the way a person would find it (the home link or a logo-named element near the top-left), and taken in its best form:
  - SVG first: inline SVG logos serialised with their real colours, an SVG offered in `<picture>` or `srcset`, or an SVG twin served beside a raster logo (`logo.png` → `logo.svg`);
  - otherwise the largest raster the page offers (PNG, WebP, JPG; GIF only as a last resort);
  - text/CSS logos (icon + name) as a 4× screenshot on true transparency.

  Without a live browser, the HTML is read for the same clues (SVG preferred, largest `srcset` entry), then structured-data logos, `og:logo` and large app icons (never a 16px favicon).

  On screen, SVG logos are rendered fresh at every size, so edges are always sharp. Raster logos are cleaned once:
  - a plain white box is keyed out, including the holes in letters, with colour-to-alpha edges and no grey outline;
  - GIF's hard 1-bit edges are smoothed;
  - small files are upscaled with high-quality filtering and never shown more than 2.5× their own pixels;
  - small copies (the corner brand bug) are scaled down in steps, so they aren't aliased.

  Only the neutral ink adapts to the style (black wordmark text goes white on dark styles, white text goes dark on light ones), so coloured marks keep their brand colours.
- **Brand polish**: an anamorphic logo reveal, a corner brand bug through the body of the video, and an end card that holds on the logo, closing line, button and URL.
- **Copy that reads like a designer wrote it**: the site's headlines are ranked for on-screen quality, the emphasis word is chosen by meaning ("*300+ tools*", "*whole team*"), bento cards carry the site's own one-line feature descriptions, and CTAs vary.
- **Claim-safe copy, always and automatically**: videos are advertising, so the words on screen and in the voice-over stay generic and descriptive, saying what the product is and does:
  - no superlatives or rankings (best, #1, leading, world's first, fastest);
  - no absolutes or guarantees (100%, guaranteed, never, always, everything);
  - no speed or multiplier claims (in seconds, instantly, 10x faster) and no comparatives with nothing to compare against (faster, better, smarter);
  - no numbers used as claims (customer counts, percentages, ratings, uptime), and no testimonials, customer-logo walls or metric scenes;
  - no efficacy or outcome promises ("stops cyber threats" → "helps you monitor cyber threats"; "boost your revenue" is left out);
  - no certification or compliance claims (SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR, "compliant", "certified", "bank-grade"), green claims (eco-friendly, sustainable, carbon neutral), endorsements ("as seen on", "recommended by") or origin claims ("Made in USA");
  - "free" only when the site itself offers something free (the FTC treats "free" as a claim that must be true).

  **Health and medical claims are removed in every mode**, following FDA rules: treats, cures, prevents, diagnoses, heals; clinically proven; FDA approved or cleared; doctor recommended; effects on sleep, stress, mood, immunity or weight.

  The site's own wording is kept where it's neutral, softened where that is safe ("The #1 CRM for startups" → "The CRM for startups"), and left out where it isn't. The built-in director's lines are written generic to begin with, and the product category is read from the site as captured, so screening never changes what kind of product it is. The same rules go into the AI director's brief, its self-review flags anything that slips through, and a final pass screens its output. Offers are reworded the same way, with nothing to confirm: "Try for Free!" → "Try it today!", "Start your free trial" → "Get started", "Sign up free" → "Sign up", and clauses about discounts, coupons, refunds or "no credit card" are left out (an end card always keeps a plain call to action). Your own edits get the same pass when you leave a field, with a short note saying what was reworded, and every export is checked once more. This is automated screening, not legal advice.
- **Takes**: "3 more takes" directs alternative cuts (product-first, proof-first, a fresh story) in parallel. They appear as live previews, and you click one to use it.
- **AI self-review**: the AI's draft is checked against a storyboard checklist covering arc, copy length, pacing, and invented quotes, logos or numbers. *Best* mode always critiques and revises its draft. *Balanced* revises only when the checklist fails. Anything invented that remains is removed.
- **Icons everywhere**: around 250 Lucide icons (ISC licence) drawn as crisp canvas vectors that draw themselves on. They appear in feature tiles, bento cards, steps, struck-out pains (a red ✕), chapter pills, notification widgets and the integrations orbit. Each icon is picked from the wording ("Invoices paid on time" gives a receipt, "Deploy in seconds" a rocket, "Close deals" a handshake), with the product category's icon family as the fallback.
- **Story arcs that adapt to the product**: Prodintro.com detects the product type from the site or prompt and uses the launch-video arc typical of it. There are 14 types: developer tool, AI, fintech, security, analytics, sales and CRM, marketing, productivity, HR, e-commerce, health, education, creative and communication. Each type sets its own beat order, whether to open on the pain or the promise, chapter labels ("Built for developers", "Works with your stack", "Security teams"), CTA voice ("Start building", "Book a demo", "Open an account", "Start selling"), icon family and a suggested style. The AI director gets the same brief, and the studio shows what it detected, with a one-click "Use suggested style".
- **New skill: Feature Icons**, the classic SaaS feature row. Glowing icon tiles draw themselves on, each with a title and a one-line benefit.
- **60-30-10 colour rule** (default for SaaS videos): 60% dominant background, 30% supporting colour for cards, panels and gradient fields, and 10% accent for highlight words, buttons, cursor and progress, in one hue. Shader gradients weight their colour spots in the same proportion, and the accent is the palette's most vivid colour. Switch to *Vibrant* in the studio for full-strength colour.
- **High-end GPU backgrounds**: animated mesh gradients, grainy gradients, silk flow, smoke rings, a neural glow and light rays, rendered frame-exactly on the GPU with the open-source [Paper Shaders](https://github.com/paper-design/shaders) (Apache-2.0). They're tinted from the palette and kept deep enough for text to stay readable. Every style has a signature background, and the studio's *Background* picker can put any gradient behind any style, or one of the five drawn Clean & Epic stages (eclipse rim, studio light, silk ribbons, light beam, colour bloom). Preview and export match exactly.
- **Animated product galleries**: the site's own images, screenshots and captured UI components, animated on the GPU with two open-source libraries, [OGL](https://github.com/oframe/ogl) and [gl-transitions](https://github.com/gl-transitions/gl-transitions):
  - *Gallery Flow*: images in sequence in a rounded frame, joined by shader transitions (cross-warp, directional warp, cross zoom, window slices, grid flip, morph, ripple, and a cube or swap on dark styles), each with a caption;
  - *3D Carousel*: the images on a curved ring that turns card by card, the front card lit with its caption and a floor reflection;
  - *Tilt Wall*: a perspective wall of product screenshots drifting behind the headline, the Linear- and Vercel-style hero look.

  The director adds a gallery beat to 20s+ videos when the page has enough imagery (a carousel for creative and e-commerce products and proof-first cuts), and story-led videos can open on the tilt wall. UI components whose shape doesn't suit the frame are shown whole on their own surface colour, never cropped through their text. Every transition is a pure function of time, so preview and export match; without WebGL, each skill falls back to 2D.
- **Every format**: all SaaS scenes are laid out for 16:9, 9:16 and 1:1.
- **Colour theory with guard rails**: on top of 60-30-10, highlight words, buttons and beams are kept at ≥3:1 contrast with the background and body text at ≥7:1 (WCAG). A dark navy brand colour on a dark style is lifted until it reads.
- **Honest copy**: KPI figures inside a product mockup are never taken as company stats, quotes drop their inline attribution, and section headings aren't mistaken for features.

## Quality: Kaizen scorecard

`npm run kaizen` generates the storyboards and scores each out of 100 (including whether each narrator line fits its scene). The corpus covers 10 websites (sales, developer tool, AI, fintech, security, e-commerce, a live capture with UI components, a sparse site, a wordy site and an Amazon product listing) and 8 prompts, across 3 lengths, 3 story angles and all 45 styles. Videos are scored on story arc (including a product-in-action moment), length accuracy, copy (length, repeats, filler endings, placeholders), variety, pacing, chapter labels, icon uniqueness, CTA and use of the site's material. It then reports the most frequent issues, so every improvement can be measured. Five cycles took the average from **88.1 to 99.0**: perfect videos rose from 2 to 504 and the lowest score from 57 to 93. Adding the interaction moments and component scenes, with a stricter scorecard, took it to **99.6**; with every style and slide added since, the corpus (now 3,408 storyboards) averages **99.6**. The remaining misses are inputs with no feature material, which get an honest shorter cut and a director's note instead of invented content.

`npm run audit:director` prints the storyboards themselves (every line on screen and the narration) for a set of SaaS, product and trailer prompts, because a saturated score can't show what reads badly. Reading them led to a round of fixes the scorecard couldn't see:
- **Names**: a name leading straight into its description is recognised ("Kelvo smart water bottle…" → *Kelvo*, not "Kelvo smart water").
- **Clean copy**: the prompt is never quoted as typed; the product is described in its own sentence ("Lumetrik is the analytics app for product teams, with…"). Openers don't repeat the name the logo reveals next ("Harbor is the AI assistant" → "The AI assistant"). "Helps brands sell online" reads as "For brands that sell online", and an audience named in the prompt joins the pitch ("Business banking for startups").
- **Labels, not fragments**: callouts drop articles and lose possessives or a leading verb rather than being cut mid-phrase ("Keeps coffee at" → "Coffee at chosen temperature").
- **No-name prompts** never say "Your product" as a name: the reveal names what it is ("Your productivity app"), the close is a plain call, and a note says where to add the name.
- **The right demo**: "developers" no longer pulls a design tool into a code-deploy demo, and prototyping, comments and hand-off count towards live cursors.
- **Product videos** don't repeat their callouts as feature cards; a video with too little material runs shorter, and its note says so.
- **Trailers** speak their style: each style has its own opening lines, beats and close, and the close fits what the trailer is for (*Subscribe now* for a channel, *Coming soon* or *Premieres* for a documentary, *Discover the collection* for a watch brand). Style and brand-type words ("gold", "toxic green", "watch brand") never become beats.
- **Store builders** close on *Start selling*, not *Shop now*.
- **Broken pictures**: a picture that fails to load counts as missing, so its slide borrows another of the video's pictures, or shows a placeholder, instead of an empty stage.

The corpus now has 10 websites, including a hype-heavy site whose copy is almost all claims, and scores 1,586 storyboards. That covers both wording modes, vertical 9:16 cuts and take variety. When the scorecard saturated, its bar was raised with what a viewer notices:
- **specificity**: headlines in the product's own words, not boilerplate;
- **rewrite damage**: fragments, dangling endings and broken plurals left by claim-safe rewriting;
- **brand**: the logo revealed when there is one, and the brand named in the closing line;
- **real product**: captured UI and imagery on screen;
- **beat sync**;
- **9:16 headline width**;
- **take variety**: "3 more takes" must give genuinely different videos;
- **claims**: none in claim-safe mode.

On the raised bar, perfect videos were 550 of 1,586. One cycle of fixes took that to **1,436**:
- product-first takes open cold on the product, with the logo after it;
- proof-first without proof becomes a value-first cut led by the product demo;
- each take opens on a different line;
- the closing narrator line always names the product;
- rewritten boasts rank below the site's clean lines;
- shortened voice lines are cut at clause boundaries;
- overlong videos drop their least essential beat.

## Deploy (Docker)

The image runs Next's standalone server with a headless Chromium for live website capture. Videos are rendered in each visitor's browser, so the server does no video work.

**What the server stores.** A website's own images and videos are streamed through `/api/asset` and never stored; the relay is needed because browsers won't export a video drawn from another site's images. Screenshots and the UI pieces cut from a page don't exist on the site, so the headless browser makes them. By default they're kept on the data volume for up to 48 hours, so share links, saved intros and admin previews show them meanwhile. A clean-up timer starts with the server (`src/instrumentation.ts`) and sweeps every 15 minutes, deleting each capture before its 48 hours are up, even when nobody imports anything; one past its time is never served. The studio says so under the player whenever a video uses screenshots or added photos: download the video when it's ready, since afterwards intros (saved ones and share links included) show without them, while images used straight from the website keep showing because they load from the site. The studio also caches captures it loads in the visitor's own browser (IndexedDB); that copy is dropped 48 hours after it was first saved too, so screenshots stop showing everywhere. Set `INTROMAKER_CAPTURE_STORAGE=browser` to keep none on the server at all.

```bash
docker build -t intromaker .
docker run -p 3000:3000 -v intromaker-data:/data intromaker     # http://localhost:3000
```

**Render**: New → Blueprint → this repo. `render.yaml` defines one web service from the Dockerfile, a 1 GB persistent disk at `/data`, the health check and the settings below. Disks need a paid instance (Starter or above); on the free plan remove the `disk` block and captures last until the next restart.

**Railway**: New Project → Deploy from GitHub repo. `railway.json` builds the Dockerfile and sets the health check; Railway supplies `PORT` and the server listens on it. Then add a Volume mounted at `/data` (captured screenshots), generate a public domain under Settings → Networking, and set any of the variables below (`INTROMAKER_MAX_CAPTURES=1` on small instances). No `RAILWAY_RUN_UID` is needed: hosts mount volumes owned by root, so the container starts as root only long enough for `docker-entry.js` to hand `/data` to the `node` user, then serves as `node`. (The Dockerfile has no `VOLUME` line, which Railway's builder rejects.)

Fly.io or any VPS run the same image; mount a volume at `/data`. Give the instance at least 1 GB of memory for comfortable live capture (each headless browser session uses about 300 MB); 512 MB works with one capture at a time.

Set these as environment variables on your host (Railway: the service → **Variables** → **New Variable** → Deploy; Render: **Environment**; Fly.io: `fly secrets set`; Docker: `--env-file`; locally: `.env.local`). **Admin → Setup → Environment variables** has the same steps for each host and shows which ones are set on the running server (never their values); the *Admin is off* page explains how to add `ADMIN_PASSWORD`.

|---|---|---|

| `INTROMAKER_DATA_DIR` | `/data` in the image | Where captured screenshots are stored; mount a persistent volume here |
| `INTROMAKER_SHOT_TTL_HOURS` / `INTROMAKER_SHOT_MAX_MB` | 48 / 1024 | Captures are deleted within this many hours (48 at most, as the studio tells visitors; lower it to delete sooner); the folder is held under this size, oldest first |
| `INTROMAKER_CAPTURE_STORAGE` | `server` | `browser`: website screenshots are never written to disk. The server holds them in memory only while the video is made (`INTROMAKER_MEMORY_TTL_MIN`, 30), and each visitor's browser keeps its own copy. Share links opened elsewhere, saved intros on another device and admin previews then show the video without them |
| `INTROMAKER_MAX_CAPTURES` | 2 | Headless browser sessions at once (about 300 MB each); more wait briefly, then fall back to reading the HTML |
| `INTROMAKER_CAPTURE_TIMEOUT_S` | 60 | Seconds one website capture may take (15 to 120) before the import reads the page's HTML instead |
| `INTROMAKER_PROXY_HOPS` | 1 | Trusted reverse proxies in front; the client address is read from `X-Forwarded-For` counted from the right, so it can't be spoofed. Use 0 when the server faces the internet directly |

| `INTROMAKER_LOGIN_TRIES` / `INTROMAKER_LOGIN_BLOCK_MIN` | 3 / 60 | Wrong passwords allowed before an address is blocked from signing in, and the block's length in minutes |

| `INTROMAKER_AI_PROVIDER` / `INTROMAKER_AI_KEY` | none | The AI director for visitors without their own key, any provider by id (`openrouter`, `deepseek`, `openai`, `gemini`, `anthropic`, `groq`, `custom`…) and its key. **Payments with Stripe (plug and play)**. No Stripe SDK and no secret API key on the server: Stripe-hosted Payment Links, one signed webhook and the customer portal. **Admin → Billing** shows the exact URLs to paste into Stripe, a checklist that ticks itself off, the mode (test or live) and the last event received.

1. In Stripe, create the product "Prodintro.com Pro" with a recurring price (monthly, and optionally yearly).
2. Create a **Payment Link** for each price. Under *After payment*, redirect to `https://<your-domain>/account?upgraded=1`, and add `https://<your-domain>/terms` as the terms link. Paste the links into Admin → Billing, or set them as environment variables (`STRIPE_MONTHLY_LINK`, `STRIPE_YEARLY_LINK`, `STRIPE_PORTAL_LINK`, `STRIPE_YEARLY_PRICE`), which win over Admin and show there read-only. Each visitor is sent to the link with their account id (`client_reference_id`) and email prefilled.
3. **Developers → Webhooks → Add endpoint** `https://<your-domain>/api/stripe/webhook` with the events listed in Admin → Billing (copy button): `checkout.session.completed`, `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`, `checkout.session.async_payment_failed`, `customer.subscription.created`, `customer.subscription.updated`, `customer.subscription.deleted`, `invoice.paid`, `invoice.payment_failed`, `charge.refunded` and `charge.dispute.created`. Copy its signing secret into the `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` variable (Railway → Variables) and redeploy.
4. Optional but recommended: **Settings → Billing → Customer portal**, activate it and paste its login link into Admin → Billing. Subscribers then get *Manage billing* to change cards, get invoices or cancel.
5. Test with test-mode links and card `4242 4242 4242 4242`, then swap in the live links and the live signing secret.

What happens: a paid checkout makes the account Pro; after the redirect the account page waits for the webhook and shows *Pro is on*. A failed renewal marks the account past due and keeps Pro while Stripe retries; a cancelled or unpaid subscription returns it to Free. A plan the owner set by hand stays until the customer's next purchase (renewals and cancellations don't change it). A checkout only counts once it's paid (a bank debit waits for `async_payment_succeeded`), `invoice.paid` clears a failed payment, and a late retry of an older event is skipped, so it can't re-grant Pro after a cancellation. A full refund moves a Stripe-paid account to Free by itself (and the event reminds the owner to cancel the subscription in Stripe if it's still active); a partial refund or a dispute flags the account in Users for the owner to decide. A plan set by hand isn't changed by a refund. A paying subscriber can't delete their account until they cancel in *Manage billing* (the owner deletes one only after confirming it was cancelled in Stripe), and *Manage billing* stays available after cancelling, for invoices.

**Terms and 30-day refunds**: once a Payment Link is in effect, `/terms` goes live, linked from the site's footers, the Pro pricing card and the account page's Upgrade buttons. It promises a full refund of a payment asked for within 30 days (first payment or renewal), says how to ask (the contact email from Admin → Plans), and covers cancelling, what you may import, and claims. Without payments the page answers 404, and the refund links, the privacy page's paragraph about Stripe and the refund note in Admin → Billing are hidden. The window is `REFUND_DAYS` in `src/lib/stripe-links.ts`.

**Admin → Billing** also lists the recent events, highlighting money that reached no account (a payment link opened directly with another email) and refunds or disputes; **Users → Link Stripe customer** attaches such a payment to its account. A *Before you sell Pro* list warns when Pro unlocks nothing over Free, when the Pro price or contact email is missing, when the data folder isn't a mounted volume (a redeploy would wipe accounts and Pro status), when the events received are from the other Stripe mode than the links, and when deliveries were refused for a bad signature. *Test it* ticks only once a paid checkout reached an account. Every event is verified (HMAC-SHA256 over the raw body, 5-minute tolerance), applied once, and a failure answers 500 so Stripe retries. Only `buy.stripe.com`, `checkout.stripe.com` and `billing.stripe.com` links are accepted.

**When a website can't be imported**, the studio says why and offers the next step instead of making a video from an error page: an unknown domain, a site that is down or refuses connections, a timeout, a bad certificate, a missing page (with a button to import the home page instead), a site that blocks automated visitors, one that is rate-limiting, a server error, a file instead of a page, and parked or placeholder domains. Without a scheme typed, `https://` is tried first, then `http://`, then the `www.` host. The messages have *Try again* where it helps and *Describe it instead*, which moves you to the prompt. An import that fails doesn't use up one of the day's imports.

**Import progress and failure reasons**: an import starts the moment you press *Make my video* on the homepage (the button says so while the studio opens). A card over the preview shows the steps (opening the website, capturing screenshots, reading copy, picking colours, directing, building the slides), a progress bar, the seconds so far and *Stop*. Slide thumbnails wait until the video is built, so the page stays responsive. If the import fails, the card says whose side the problem is on and runs a quick health check of the website (`POST /api/scrape/check`, rate-limited): whether it answered and how fast, whether it lets automated visitors in, https, a page title, readable text without scripts, and a logo or icon. A bare `502`/`503`/`504` with no message comes from the hosting service in front of Prodintro.com (the server restarted, ran out of memory or time while capturing), so it's reported as Prodintro.com's side, with the website's health shown underneath. On small servers set `INTROMAKER_MAX_CAPTURES=1`, give the service at least 1 GB of memory, and keep `INTROMAKER_CAPTURE_TIMEOUT_S` under the host's request timeout.

**What protects a public deploy**:
- **Rate limits** per visitor on import (8 per 10 min), the image proxy, generation, voice and key checks. Over the limit you get a `429` with `Retry-After`. Generation that the server's own AI key pays for also has a per-visitor and a shared daily budget, and degrades to the built-in director rather than failing.
- **Capture queue**: live captures run a bounded number of browser sessions, with a wait list and a hard timeout.
- **SSRF guard**: only public `http(s)` addresses; loopback, private, link-local, CGNAT, cloud-metadata, IPv4-mapped and NAT64 IPv6 forms and numeric IP tricks are refused. Every redirect hop is re-checked. Server-side fetches are pinned to the checked address at connect time, which defeats DNS rebinding. Live capture checks every request the page makes.
- **Local AI** (Ollama, LM Studio) is only reachable from the server when it runs on your own machine; hosted, it runs from the visitor's browser.
- **Headers**: `nosniff`, `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, a strict referrer policy, a permissions policy and HSTS; captured SVGs are served with a sandboxing CSP.
- **Keys** stay in the visitor's browser and are removed from error messages.

**Offers are reworded automatically.** Intros never promise a deal: anything free, a trial, a discount or coupon, "no credit card" or a money-back guarantee is turned into a neutral call to action or left out, in the director's copy and in your edits, without asking.

`npm run check:copy` checks the copy rules: end-card buttons worded for the intro's content, "free" only when the copy really offers something free ("hassle-free", "free up your time" and "error-free" don't count), and site buttons cleaned of arrows, emoji and shouting.

`npm run check:hosting` verifies all of this in production mode (rate limits, spoofing, the AI budget, storage clean-up, the admin area, accounts and plans, the Stripe webhook (forged, stale and repeated events, upgrades, cancellations), website-import error messages, around 30 SSRF cases including DNS rebinding, local AI).

Known limits:
- Rate limits and the AI budget are in memory, which suits a single instance. Several instances would share them through Redis behind the same `rateLimit()` interface.
- The headless browser resolves DNS itself, so for defence in depth run it where the container has no route to internal services (the default on Render, Railway and Fly).

Every skill is a deterministic function of time: it takes a seeded RNG and no per-frame state. That means scrubbing, looping previews and export all produce identical frames.

### Adding a skill

1. Write a `render(sc: SkillContext)` function in `src/engine/skills/`. Use the helpers in `fx.ts`: `background`, `headline`, `subline`, `exitT` and so on.
2. Add its id to `SKILL_IDS` in `types.ts`.
3. Register it in `skills/index.ts`. The AI Director picks it up automatically from its `bestFor` description.
